Week 0: Intake and entry task

Admission procedure before Session 1. Draft, not yet run with any learner. What you type in the boxes is saved on this device straight away. You can also write in another tool, as long as you keep the record.

  • AIntake interview
  • BEntry task: two candidate problems
  • CNote to send to your manager

1. Intake interview

One call of about 30 minutes per candidate, led by the facilitator. Notes are taken in text form, with no audio recording unless you agree. You will not be asked about salary or the names of your company’s customers.

Prepare concrete answers rather than general statements (such as “improve quality”). Here are the nine questions for the call:

  1. Purpose: “What do you want this course to change in your work over the next 6 weeks?”
  2. Current work: “What did you do last week, and for whom? Who uses what you make?”
  3. Authority to change: “Can you change the product or process that your colleagues use, or does someone need to approve? Who?”
  4. AI tools: “Which AI tools does the company allow, and is there any tool you must not put data into?”
  5. Hours: “Last week, how many hours could you really give to learning outside work hours? What about a week with a deadline?” (This number is real data for refining the self-work hours; for now it is only a provisional number.)
  6. Release calendar: “How long does a small change of yours take to get from done to the user’s hands?”
  7. Where numbers come from: “Which numbers can you see about your own work (log, ticket, report)?”
  8. Required condition: “Do you have a real piece of work to ship into?” (If not, the course cannot grade your work.)
  9. Public sharing: “Does your company have any limits on talking about this outside?”

2. Entry task

Sent after the intake interview, and due at least 3 days before the course starts.

Bring two candidate problems from your current work. For each problem, answer:

  • Who is being harmed? Name a role or a person; do not write “the whole company”.
  • How many times a week does it happen, or how much per week? If you do not know, write “not known” and say why you do not know yet.
  • Three times “why”, downward towards the cause: why does this problem happen, where each answer is the input to the next question. (The upward direction towards value, “why does the company pay for this work”, is left for Week 2.)

Write two short pieces, no more than one page per problem. Do not use AI for the three times “why”; the rest is up to you. The purpose is for you to touch first principles in the very first task, before seeing the model.

Example of the format (constructed example, not real numbers): “Problem 1: the accountant has to reconcile the report by hand every Monday. Harmed: the accountant. Frequency: once a week. Why 1: …”

3. Note to send to your manager

You send this note (or say it in person); the facilitator only provides the template and does not send it on your behalf.

I am joining a six-week course (a group of at most five people) to try one small change, measured before and after, in my own work at the company. The change will be an improvement to a tool, process or product that my colleagues use. I will not take the company’s data outside the tools the company allows. I would like you to know and to agree to let me try. I would also like to ask: when there is a result, may I share it publicly (for example in an article), or may I only present it in class? Either is fine with me; I only need to know so that I do the right thing.

Manager reply record

Record the reply as one of: agree, agree with conditions, refuse, or no reply yet. If your manager refuses or has not replied, tell the facilitator before the course starts; that is a real blocker. Public sharing is encouraged, not required.
