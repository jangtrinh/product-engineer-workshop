Learner brief for Week 6

Week 6 and the re-measure session. This is a draft, not yet run with learners. Times and minutes may change.

What you will be able to do

  • Defend your ship item aloud against five questions. You do the last question on paper, with no AI.
  • Have two other people grade your ship item, your agent team and your evidence, using records, screenshots and a live run.
  • Turn one lesson from the six weeks into an automatic check that runs.
  • Plan the re-measure and update your ontology with new decisions.
  • After 2 to 4 weeks, defend the before and after numbers of your ship item.

Deadline

The ship item must reach real users by the end of week 6 at the latest. Bring a record or a screenshot that shows this.

The five questions of the oral defence

You know the five questions in advance, in this order:

  1. Explain the most important decision of the ship item.
  2. Which option was rejected, and why.
  3. Which part the AI did; open your AI work log to point to it.
  4. What would make your conclusion wrong.
  5. Redo a small, similar part on paper, with no AI. You do not know the task yet.

Each person has 20 minutes. The facilitator (in week 6, the owner) asks the questions.

What to bring

  • The ship item, the before and after measurement table, and the start date of the “after” window.
  • Your AI work log, following the class convention: each time you give a task to the AI, write what you gave, what the AI returned, and how you checked it. Keep it open so you can point to it quickly. Hand in this log, together with the spec history, to the facilitator 24 hours before the oral defence, so the facilitator can choose the item to ask about in question 3.
  • Paper and a pen. The laptop will be closed for question 5.
  • The record of your agent team run, and the record of one red run and one green run of the gate.
  • The evidence pack handed in 24 hours before the peer-grading session (Session 2): a record or screenshot showing the ship item reached real users, the measurement table, the record of the agent team and the gate, and the table of who does it, who approves, who is accountable. Company-sensitive data removed.

The re-measure session

The date of this session is fixed when everyone has an “after” window as long as their own baseline window. You have 20 minutes. Preparation, handed in 48 hours before:

  • The measurement table (raw numbers) before and after, with the start and end dates of each window.
  • The way you measured; if you changed the way you measured, write the date of the change and the reason.
  • What you could not measure. Write “not measured”, do not estimate to make it look good.
  • The value and the cost of one unit, compared with the week 2 estimate; and the agent running cost per unit, compared separately with the number you wrote in week 4.
  • Your decision: keep, change or remove the change.

You will rerun the calculation from the measurement table right there in the room.

If you are stuck

  • You have not shipped to real users in time: tell the owner before the session, with evidence of what you have done and the reason. Do not wait until the session.
  • Your company blocks exporting the AI log: copy it out following the convention. A missing trace because of missing access is not evidence of anything, but you must still be able to explain the part you did.
  • You cannot think of a lesson to turn into a check: start from the mistake that cost you the most time.
  • You are not sure the “after” window is long enough: write the number you have and tell the owner honestly.

Session 1: defence preparation sheet

You know the five questions in advance. Write your answers here ahead of time, then bring them to the session. You do not know the task for question 5 yet.

Your answers to questions 1 to 4

No AI

Each person has 20 minutes. You answer the five questions aloud. Question 5, the redo on paper, is done with no AI and the laptop closed.

Question 1: Explain the most important decision of the ship item

The facilitator says: “Choose one decision such that, if you had done it differently, the ship item would be different. Explain that decision to someone who has never heard about your work.”

Question 2: Which option was rejected, and why

The facilitator says: “Besides the way you chose, which other way did you consider? Why did you reject it?”

Question 3: Which part the AI did, open the log and point

The facilitator says: “Open the AI work log. Show me one task you gave, what the AI returned, and how you checked it.” You open the log only to point to it, not to ask the AI more.

Question 4: What would make your conclusion wrong

The facilitator says: “What, if it happened, would make your conclusion about this ship item wrong? What would you measure to know?”

Question 5: Redo a small, similar part, on paper, with no AI

You do not know the task yet. The facilitator will say: “Close the laptop, put the phone away. Here is a small task like the part you did. Do it on paper, say aloud what you are thinking. I will not give hints. You have six minutes.”
