Case-study permission letter

Draft template, not yet used for real. This template is not legal advice. If your employment contract or security policy says something clearer, follow that. Telling your case study publicly is encouraged, not required: if the company does not agree, you only present in class and you still count as completing the course like everyone else, if there is enough evidence.

  • ABefore sending
  • BContent of the letter
  • CAfter receiving the reply

1. Before sending

  1. Write a draft case study with the company’s data removed: no customer names, no internal numbers that have not been announced, no screenshots with private information, no colleagues’ names unless they agree.
  2. Choose how to tell the number: increase or decrease as a ratio, or a range, instead of an absolute number, if the absolute number is sensitive.
  3. Send it to your direct manager or the person in charge of communications, security or legal, depending on the company. Send it from your work email, not through a personal channel.

2. Content of the letter

Copy the template below and fill in the square brackets before sending.

Subject: Request for permission to share publicly a version of [short name of the change] with company data removed

Hello [name of recipient],

Over the past six weeks I took part in the Product Engineer Workshop course and made a change in my work: [one sentence describing the change, with no sensitive data]. [If the recipient is the direct manager: You were told about this from the start of the course. If the recipient is the communications, security or legal department: My direct manager is [name] and was told about this from the start of the course.]

I would like to ask permission to tell publicly a version with all of the company’s data removed. This version will talk about how I framed the problem, how I measured before and after, and what I took from it. This version will not have: the company name and customer names, absolute internal numbers, source code, screenshots with private information, colleagues’ names.

Planned places to tell it: [a blog or LinkedIn post / a talk / in a later cohort of the course / only in class, choose one or more].

I attach the draft for you to look at first. I will not publish it before I have your reply, and I will edit or remove any part you want.

If the company does not want it told publicly yet, I fully understand; I will only present in class. This does not affect my work at the company.

I hope you can reply before [date].

Thank you.
[your name]

3. After receiving the reply

ReplyWhat to do
Agrees in writingKeep the reply letter in your own private place. Tell the owner “allowed to tell publicly” with the scope (where, with or without absolute numbers). You do not need to send the whole letter to the owner.
Agrees with conditionsDo exactly the conditions (remove a passage, change a number); ask for confirmation again of the edited version before publishing.
Does not agree or does not replyTreat it as no. Present only in class.

Publish nothing without a clear agreement. Silence is not agreement.

Your permission request record

Keep track of whom you asked, when you sent the draft, and the scope they agreed to.
